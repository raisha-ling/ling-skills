# Archetypes — deciding which 30 minutes to run

Classify before writing questions. The archetype sets the time weighting; the weighting is the whole value of the prep.

Classify from **behaviour**, not from how the user was recruited. Two signals decide it:

- **Engagement** — lifetime usage minutes, sessions, lessons completed, days since last activity.
- **Incident** — did something go wrong to them (billing, access, bug, bad content)?

---

## The five

### A. Paid & never activated
*Bought, then barely used it. Low minutes, low lessons, subscription live.*

The most valuable and most commonly mis-run interview. Easy to waste on feature wishlists from someone who has seen almost nothing of the product.

**Weight:** 25% activation breakdown · 25% why they stopped · 15% purchase decision · 20% the little they did see · 15% context/JTBD.

**Must-ask:** what they expected when they paid; the specific moment they put it down; what would have to be true to open it tomorrow.

**Don't:** ask them to evaluate features they never reached.

---

### B. Incident-damaged
*Something went wrong — double charge, lockout, lost progress, refund fight.*

Overlaps A often. The interview's job is establishing whether the incident **caused** the disengagement or merely coincided with it. Those need opposite fixes: trust repair vs. product/habit work.

**Weight:** 40% forensic reconstruction of the incident · 20% trust state · 20% disengagement · 20% context.

**Must-ask:** what they actually saw on screen, step by step; how long they waited before acting; the 1–10 trust scale, **then and now**; whether they considered a chargeback; how support landed.

**Don't:** reveal what you already know about the cause. Ask what they saw. Never defend.

---

### C. Power user
*High minutes, long streak, deep into content, often multi-language.*

Rarest and worth different questions entirely. They can evaluate the product properly because they've actually used it.

**Weight:** 30% workflow and habit · 25% what's missing at their depth · 20% what nearly made them quit · 15% referral/word-of-mouth · 10% context.

**Must-ask:** walk me through a typical session; where does Ling stop being enough; what do you use alongside it.

---

### D. Churned / cancelled
*Subscription ended by their choice.*

**Weight:** 35% the decision moment · 20% what they switched to · 20% the trajectory before the decision · 15% what would bring them back · 10% context.

**Must-ask:** where were you and what were you doing when you decided; how long had it been coming; what are you using now, and what does it do better.

---

### E. Happy but shallow
*Positive sentiment, modest consistent usage, no incident.*

Least dramatic, quietly the most representative of the base.

**Weight:** 30% habit mechanics — what makes a session happen or not · 25% what they'd miss if it vanished · 20% unmet needs · 15% the sharing question · 10% context.

**Must-ask:** what triggers a session; what makes you skip a day; what would you tell a friend it's for.

---

## Universal spine

Whatever the archetype, keep these:

| Slot | Purpose |
|---|---|
| First 2–3 min | Frame it. Not a support call, not a sales call. Critical feedback is more useful than praise. Ask to record. |
| Next ~4 min | Why this language. Warms them up *and* gets the real JTBD — deadline-driven vs. aspirational, which changes everything. |
| Final 2 min | "If you were me, what's the first thing you'd fix?" · incentive logistics · permission to follow up in a month. |

Always ask for the month-later follow-up. Whether they actually came back is the cheapest retention signal available.

## Question craft

- **Bold the verbatim asks.** Leave probes unbolded — they're only used if the user is brief.
- **Past behaviour, not future intent.** "Walk me through the last time you opened it" beats "would you use a feature that…". People are unreliable about hypotheticals and reliable about last Tuesday.
- **One question at a time.** Stacked questions get the easiest one answered.
- **Scales earn their place after a story, not before.** Get the narrative, then anchor it: "1 to 10, where were you?"
- **80/20.** They talk. Note it in the doc so the interviewer sees it live.

## Common cross-archetype traps

Carried in the skill body — incentive bias, mistaken-for-support, unanswered-message, don't-reveal-the-defect, don't-quote-their-usage, never-defend, don't-infer-literacy-from-age, use silence.
